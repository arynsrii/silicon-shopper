## LinkedIn project entry (current)

**Silicon Shopper | Independent Research in AI, Consumer Behavior & Behavioral Economics**

I was curious whether an AI shopping assistant would react to familiar marketing tactics the way people sometimes do.

I built a Python experiment with 40 fictional product categories and ran 960 model calls to test four effects: decoy pricing, price anchoring, scarcity and the trade-off between star ratings and review volume.

One result surprised me. In my setup, the model largely *avoided* products labeled “Only 2 left in stock.” That raised a new question: does an AI buyer interpret the same scarcity message as urgency, or as a potential delivery risk?

The first version tests one model, so I am treating these as preliminary findings. I am now designing stronger comparisons and a reproducible research workflow around that question.

Code and methods: https://github.com/arynsrii/silicon-shopper
